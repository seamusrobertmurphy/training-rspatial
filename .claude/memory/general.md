- 2026-09-06: CARB's Compliance Offset Protocol U.S. Forest Projects, adopted 25 June 2015, was not in the standards library and is now at `references/standards/CARB/forestprotocol2015.pdf`, 146 pages. Appendix D sets the reversal risk rating that fixes the Forest Buffer Account contribution. Table D.7 gives wildfire 2 per cent where fire risk reduction work is confirmed in writing by the responsible fire protection agency and 4 per cent where it is not; Table D.8 gives disease and insects a flat 3 per cent for every United States project; Table D.9 gives other episodic catastrophic events a flat 3 per cent. Equation D.1 combines the eight risk types multiplicatively. Matters because it corrects the standing assumption that CARB treats a failure to map beetle or fire risk as an intentional reversal: it doubles a buffer contribution for fire and does nothing at all for insects, and the intentional against unintentional split turns on who caused the reversal, not on baseline due diligence.
- 2026-09-06: the intentional and unintentional reversal definitions are not in the CARB protocol. It defers to Cap-and-Trade Regulation section 95802 for the definitions and 95983 for the disposition of a project after a reversal, and neither is in the library. Four retrieval attempts from the Air Resources Board site on 2026-09-06 returned nothing. Matters because the wording of both definitions is unconfirmed and must not be quoted. The nearest confirmed wording is the protocol's own definition of an Intentional Back Burn at paragraph 29, which turns on "no negligence, gross negligence, or willful misconduct of the forest owner", and that triple is probably the same test, but that is an inference.
- 2026-09-06: the IPCC natural disturbance disaggregation guidance is the 2019 Refinement, Volume 4, Chapter 2, Section 2.6, in `references/standards/IPCC-md`. Box 2.2k, after EU Regulation 2018/841, builds a wildfire emissions series from 1971 (base year minus twenty), iteratively strips values above the mean plus two standard deviations until the distribution is normal, and calls the surviving mean the background level and twice its standard deviation the margin. Its footnote gives the reason a long record is needed: two standard deviations approximates Student's t only for series of thirty or more. Australia instead uses the 95 per cent probability level confined to states with abnormal fire activity, and Canada a physical threshold of all stand-replacing fire plus anything killing more than 20 per cent of biomass. Matters because three jurisdictions disagree about which years were natural, and because the thirty is a sample size rather than a round number, which is the book's spine asked along the time axis.
