# Book Proposal

**Working title:** Spatial Analysis in R for Forest Carbon Verification
**Subtitle:** A Practical Training in GIS
**Author:** Seamus Murphy (ORCID 0000-0002-1792-0351)
**Format:** Quarto book, HTML and print co-edition
**Status:** Course taught and drafted; full manuscript to be written under contract

## The book in one paragraph

Carbon projects are audited on numbers that come out of a GIS, and almost nobody
who audits them was trained to build one. This book teaches the spatial analysis
that forest-carbon verification actually rests on, from reading a shapefile to
reporting an area-adjusted emission with a stated uncertainty, entirely in R and
entirely reproducibly. It runs nine to sixteen chapters on one mapped forest plot,
so a single dataset carries the reader from raw coordinates through vector and
raster analysis, terrain, LiDAR, disturbance detection and uncertainty
propagation, and each chapter closes on a worked example from a real verification
project under VM0048, ACR or IPCC Tier 1 rules.

## The gap

The R-spatial shelf is well served on method and empty on application to carbon
markets. Geocomputation with R and Spatial Data Science teach the tools superbly
and stop at the discipline boundary. Introduction to Forestry Data Analysis with
R covers inventory and biometrics, not registry accounting. On the other side,
the methodological guidance that carbon projects are actually held to, the Verra
and ACR modules, the IPCC good-practice guidance, the Olofsson area-adjustment
literature, exists as standards documents and journal papers with no
computational teaching text and no runnable code.

The result is a workforce gap. Verification bodies, project developers and
national MRV teams hire analysts who can either do GIS or read a methodology,
rarely both, and the reconciliation happens in undocumented spreadsheets. This
book is the missing bridge: it takes the standard R-spatial curriculum and drives
every chapter to a number a verifier would accept.

No competing title was found in a search of the CRC, Springer, Elsevier, Wiley
and Cambridge catalogues. The nearest neighbours are listed below.

## Audience

Primary, in order of size:

1. Analysts and technical staff at carbon project developers and verification
   bodies. This is a growing professional readership that currently trains on
   the job.
2. Graduate students in forestry, environmental science and remote sensing,
   particularly on MRV, carbon accounting and applied geospatial courses.
3. National forest monitoring and REDD+ teams, where the same workflows are run
   under UNFCCC reporting rules.

Secondary: consultants and auditors moving into carbon from adjacent fields, and
practitioners in agriculture and biodiversity credits, where the spatial and
uncertainty machinery is identical and only the methodology differs.

The book assumes introductory statistics and no prior GIS. It suits both a
one-semester taught course and self-study.

## Distinguishing features

1. One running dataset across the whole book, so concepts compound instead of
   restarting at every chapter.
2. Every chapter closes on a real verification workflow: VM0048 risk, ACR
   accuracy assessment, IPCC Tier 1 fire emissions, area-adjusted accuracy.
3. Fully reproducible. The book is a Quarto project; every figure and number in
   the text is generated by code that ships with it and runs offline.
4. Uncertainty is treated as a first-class subject with its own chapter, matching
   how carbon claims are actually challenged in audit.
5. Written by a practising analyst, drawing on published point-process research
   in post-disturbance forest regeneration.

## Table of contents

The nine chapters below are drafted. The proposal is to expand them into a full
textbook, adding the depth, exercises, solutions and case material a taught
course needs, and adding the chapters marked "new".

**Part I: Foundations**

1. Getting Started. Reproducible projects, the spatial stack, vector versus
   raster, a first map, the coordinate sanity check that opens every project.
2. Vector Data. Geometry types, attributes and support, spatial joins, validity
   and area. Closes on an area check.
3. Raster Data. The grid model, map algebra, reclassification, extraction to
   points and polygons. Closes on land-cover reclassification.
4. Coordinate Reference Systems in Depth (new). Datums, projections, distortion,
   and the transformation errors that silently move a project boundary.

**Part II: The physical layers**

5. Terrain and Hydrology. Elevation models, slope and aspect, hillshade, flow
   direction and accumulation. Closes on inundation mapping.
6. LiDAR and Point Clouds. Returns, ground classification, DTM and CHM
   construction, individual tree detection. Closes on a LiDAR forest inventory.
7. Optical Remote Sensing and Indices (new). Sensors, atmospheric correction,
   vegetation indices, and what an index can and cannot support in a claim.
8. Classification and Land Cover (new). Supervised classification, training
   design, class definition against a methodology's land-cover categories.

**Part III: Inference and error**

9. Disturbance and Risk. Change detection, accuracy assessment, spatial risk
   modelling. Closes on VM0048 risk and ACR accuracy.
10. Uncertainty. Sampling error, Monte Carlo propagation, conservative
    estimation. Closes on area-adjusted accuracy after Olofsson.
11. Sampling Design for Monitoring (new). Stratification, sample size, permanent
    versus temporary plots, and the design constraints registries impose.
12. Allometry and Biomass (new). Equation selection, propagation of allometric
    error into the carbon estimate, and why model choice dominates the error
    budget.

**Part IV: Capstones and practice**

13. Time Series. Space-time cubes, temporal reduction, trend estimation, event
    detection. Closes on IPCC Tier 1 fire emissions.
14. Spatial Patterns. Point patterns, intensity, nearest neighbour, Ripley's K.
    Closes on post-fire conifer regeneration from published field data.
15. Building a Verifiable Workflow (new). Project structure, automation,
    versioning, and producing an audit trail a verifier can follow.
16. Standards and Registry Practice. Spatial data protocols, ISO governance, and
    ART-TREES, Verra and ACR requirements in practice.

Appendices: package reference, data sources, and solutions to exercises.

## Schedule

Six months from contract to full manuscript, assuming the drafted chapters are
expanded first and the new chapters written in the second half. The
existing Quarto infrastructure means production is largely automated: the source
generates print and web from one set of files.

## Online edition

The book is written in Quarto and the intention is to keep a free HTML edition
online alongside the paid print edition, on the model of Geocomputation with R
and Introduction to Forestry Data Analysis with R. This is a requirement rather
than a preference: the free edition is what reaches the practitioner audience in
countries where the MRV work is actually done, and it drives print sales rather
than displacing them.
