# Publisher routes and contacts

Researched 30 July 2026. Verify addresses against the live pages before sending;
several were decoded from obfuscated mailto links and staff move often.

## 1. Chapman & Hall / CRC, The R Series (lead)

The best fit, and the only route verified to allow a permanently free online
edition alongside paid print.

Proposal inbox, from the series page at
routledge.com/Chapman--HallCRC-The-R-Series/book-series/CRCTHERSER:

- david.grubbs@taylorandfrancis.com, Senior Editor, Data Science and Statistics,
  remit explicitly covers books in R and environmental statistics
- lara.spieker@tandf.co.uk, Editor, Biostatistics, Computational and
  Environmental Statistics
- rob.calver@tandf.co.uk, Editor, Mathematics, Physics, Statistics

Adjacent editors, from routledge.com/contacts/editorial:

- hannah.ferguson@tandf.co.uk, agriculture and environmental sciences, ecology
- kyra.lindholm@taylorandfrancis.com, named contact for open-access book ideas
- irma.shagla@taylorandfrancis.com, GIS, remote sensing and cartography. The page
  shows a display name that does not match this address, so treat it as a legacy
  alias and confirm before using.

Series editors are John Chambers, Torsten Hothorn, Duncan Temple Lang and Hadley
Wickham. Currency uncertain; the series page no longer lists them. Do not
approach them cold, ask the commissioning editor.

Process: no published proposal checklist. The editor sends a subject-specific
form after first contact. One editor at a time. External peer review takes a
minimum of four weeks. Completed manuscripts are welcome. Printing runs four to
six weeks after production clears.

Free online edition: not written policy, but consistent practice. Yihui Xie
reports that Chapman & Hall asks only for print and sale rights and authors keep
the free online version. Geocomputation with R, R Markdown: The Definitive Guide
and Introduction to Forestry Data Analysis with R are all CRC titles with free
full text. Get the retained web rights written into the contract rather than
relying on custom.

In-flight competition in this series is heavy: Introduction to Forestry Data
Analysis with R (Finley and Doser, September 2026, 630pp, free at ifdar-book.org)
and Spatial Data Science with Applications in R (Pebesma and Bivand, September
2026). Lead with the registry and verification layer, which none of them touch.

## 2. Elsevier, Earth Observation series (fallback)

Tightest scope match after CRC, and the only series found with a named academic
editor who invites direct contact.

- gpetropoulos@hua.gr, Professor George P. Petropoulos, Harokopio University,
  series editor
- p.llewellyn@elsevier.com, acquisitions editor (first name not printed on the
  page)

Also relevant, Developments in Environmental Science: p.sadhukhan@elsevier.com,
Pamela Sadhukhan, acquisitions.

General route: the Elsevier Book Proposal General Form 2026, linked from
elsevier.com/publishing/publish-a-book, returned to AuthorQuery@elsevier.com with
the subject line "Proposal in [subject area] / [author name: working title]". It
asks for audience, competition, chapter-level contents, timeline and three named
reviewers with email addresses. The form states that generative AI must not be
used to prepare the proposal.

Closest competing title anywhere: Spatial Modeling in GIS and R for Earth and
Environmental Sciences (Pourghasemi), ISBN 978-0-12-815226-3.

## 3. Springer, Use R!

Series editors Robert Gentleman, Kurt Hornik, Giovanni Parmigiani.

Contacts: faith.su@springer.com and veronika.rosteck@springer.com, the latter
also named as publishing editor for the series. The series page points to a
regional editor list at springer.com/gp/statistics/contact-us, which now
redirects to a generic landing page. The live route is
booksubmarine.springernature.com/link/publish-a-book.

The Book Proposal Form is mandatory:
media.springer.com/full/springer-instructions-for-authors-assets/pdf/SN_BPF_EN.pdf
It requires a biography and CV, originality statement, page and figure counts,
chapter-by-chapter contents with three or four sentences each, a 3000-character
description, five to twenty keywords, three to five unique selling points at 120
characters, audience, competing titles with comparison, and open-access intent.
Springer asks for sole consideration, so do not run this in parallel with CRC.

Springer's position on a permanently free full text alongside paid print is
unverified. Ask directly before investing in the form.

Comparables: Applied Spatial Data Analysis with R, Forest Analytics with R,
Numerical Ecology with R, R Coding for Ecology (Rocchini, 2026).

## 4. Cambridge University Press

Ruled out unless the free online edition is abandoned or Gold OA is paid for.
CUP's Green OA policy states it does not apply to textbooks or professional books
for practitioners, "no part of which may be posted online without our prior
approval".

For reference: proposals go by email to a subject editor, no online form.
authorhub@cambridge.org is generic; named editors include emma.kiddle@cambridge.org,
susie.francis@cambridge.org, matt.lloyd@cambridge.org, anna.scriven@cambridge.org.
Uniquely, CUP asks textbook proposals for an example syllabus and a statement of
which courses the book suits, which would suit material that began as training.
The Topics in Remote Sensing series appears dormant, last title 1993.

## 5. Wiley

Route is a form, not a person:
wiley.qualtrics.com/jfe/form/SV_d125Tq4Ezrtwa2O

Requires a CV, full contents with subdivisions, a sample chapter if available,
and a competitive analysis with strengths and weaknesses. Two of its questions
fit this book well: whether a graduate text is primary or supplemental, and which
professional societies form the readership in order of importance. Wiley targets
six to twelve months for manuscript development. The only public editor list
found dates to February 2017 and should not be relied on.

Shelf competition is heaviest here: Lillesand, Remote Sensing and Image
Interpretation, 7th ed., and Longley, Geographic Information Science and Systems,
4th ed.

## Commercial expectations

No publisher discloses royalty rates. Taylor & Francis states only the structure:
a percentage of net income, one rate across print and ebook. Anecdotal
comparators from technical publishers run 5 to 15 per cent of net, with Scott
Meyers reporting 10 to 18 per cent as a normal band for new technical authors,
though that source is old.

Advances are largely absent. The Taylor & Francis contracts FAQ lists royalties,
free copies and an author discount, and does not mention an advance. A free
online co-edition makes one less likely still, since print becomes the
publisher's only revenue. Do not plan on income from this book; plan on it as
credentialing and reach.

Open-access alternatives are expensive. Springer Nature OA monographs start at
about EUR 12,905, Taylor & Francis OA books at about GBP 11,000 for humanities
monographs with STEM and textbooks priced separately and unpublished. Open Book
Publishers charges no fee but its remit is humanities and social sciences, so fit
is poor. Amazon KDP pays 70 per cent only between USD 2.99 and 12.99, which is
below textbook pricing, so self-publishing at a realistic price drops to 35 per
cent.

## Realistic timeline

Publisher-stated figures: peer review four weeks minimum, printing four to six
weeks after production. Real-world comparator: Geocomputation with R took about
nine months from idea to contract, about two years to write, and reached print
roughly two and a half years after contract.

For this book, assume one to three months to a reviewed contract, twelve to
fifteen months of writing to expand nine drafted chapters to sixteen, and five to
seven months in production. The existing Quarto infrastructure compresses
production considerably.
